Snack test 2026

Research Methods and Statistics, demonstration in class on 12 November 2026

Is nationality associated with how you feel about Dutch snacks? 232 of you answered. For each snack, we test H0 (nationality and opinion are independent) against HA (they are dependent), with α = 0.01, chosen before the data came in. Each figure shows the conditional proportions per nationality, and the X2 distribution with the critical value (black arrow) and our X2 (purple arrow).

Why only the right tail?

The X2 test is always two-sided. Differences between the observed and the expected counts, in either direction, make X2 larger, which is why only one tail (the right one) is extreme. Based on a significant X2, we can therefore only make a two-sided conclusion (there is an association, not a “positive” or “negative” one). The conditional proportions then describe how the groups differ in our sample.

Conditional proportions

  • P(Love | Dutch) = 114/116 = 0.983
  • P(Love | Non-Dutch) = 108/116 = 0.931
  • Difference = 0.052, relative risk = 0.983 / 0.931 = 1.06

Because p > 0.01, we fail to reject H0. There is no evidence for an association between nationality and the opinion on Stroopwafels (absence of evidence).
