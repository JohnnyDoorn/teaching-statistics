Moderation

Moderation

In statistics and regression analysis, moderation occurs when the relationship between two variables depends on a third variable. The third variable is referred to as the moderator variable or simply the moderator. The effect of a moderating variable is characterized statistically as an interaction.

Source WIKIPEDIA

Centering

  • With an interaction, \(b_1\) and \(b_2\) are effects when the other predictor is 0
  • Grand mean centering subtracts the mean from every score, so 0 now means average
  • Leaves \(b_3\) alone, but changes interpretation of \(b_1\) and \(b_2\)
Not centered Centered
\(b_1\) (hours.awake) -3.612, at 0 ml coffee 23.013, at the mean (260 ml)
\(b_2\) (ml.coffee) 0.266, at 0 hours awake 1.269, at the mean (9.8 hours)
\(b_3\) (interaction) 0.102 0.102
  • Impact of centering grows as the interaction effect grows

Recap

  • Interactions still matter with continuous predictors
  • Continuous interactions can be assessed with:
    • Linear regression
    • Process module

Exam note

  • Keep the default settings of each analysis, no need to center by hand
    • Process module centers for you (Mean-centered moderation)
    • Linear regression does not center
  • So \(b_1\) and \(b_2\) differ between the two analyses
    • Process gives the effect at the mean of the other predictor
    • Linear regression gives the effect when the other predictor is 0
  • The interaction \(b_3\) is the same in both
  • Check the assumptions in Linear Regression, not in the Process module
    • VIF in the model without the interaction
    • Checking the assumptions (lecture 21)
