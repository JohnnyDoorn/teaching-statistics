21. Multiple regression

27 October 2026 · Recording · Slides · Handout

We open block 3 (life is continuous) with multiple regression, predicting album sales from two continuous predictors, advertisement budget and airplay. In the applet we see how every predictor we add changes the model predictions and the explained variance (R²), and how the regression coefficients are tested. Then we go through the assumptions, with extra attention to multicollinearity and the VIF, and meet the bootstrap as a robust alternative. After the break we picture the model as a plane in three dimensions, compute its predictions and errors, and use hierarchical regression to isolate what a single predictor adds (the R² change). We finish with the full analysis in JASP, assumption checks included.

22. Mediation

28 October 2026 · Recording · Slides · Handout

This lecture is about mediation, where the association between a predictor and an outcome runs (partly) through a third variable, the mediator. We start from multiple regression drawn as a path model, split the shared variance into a direct and an indirect part, and work through the presumed media influence example (does a front-page article change people’s intention to buy sugar, through how much media influence they perceive?). We fit the three regressions ourselves, combine the a and b paths into the indirect effect (a × b), and test it. After the break we discuss why a mediation model needs an experimental design before it supports causal claims (equivalent models), run the analysis in JASP’s Process module, and return to the applet to separate associated predictors from an interaction.

26. Moderation

6 November 2026 · Recording · Slides · Handout

Moderation is an interaction between continuous predictors, where the effect of one predictor depends on the value of another. With the coffee example (wakefulness predicted from hours awake and milliliters of coffee) we look at the regression equation with an interaction term, at the model in three dimensions and in a descriptives plot with separate lines, and at the predictions the model makes. We run the analysis in the Process module, including the conditional effects for three values of the moderator. After the break we test the interaction with hierarchical regression (does it add explained variance?), look at centering and at how Process and linear regression differ, check the assumptions in JASP, and use the applet to tell associated predictors apart from an interaction.

28. Chi-squared test

11 November 2026 · Recording · Slides · Handout

The chi-squared test is about the association between two categorical variables, so we work with counts. With this year’s class data (preferred way of watching the lecture, and personality) we predict the count in every cell under the null hypothesis of independence, compare those predictions with the observed counts, and add everything up into the χ² statistic and its p-value. We check the expected counts (with Fisher’s exact test as the alternative when they are small) and use the standardized residuals to see where the null model is most wrong. The effect size is the odds ratio, which we build up from conditional probabilities and odds. After the break we run the analysis in JASP, add a third variable as a layer, use the test to check whether predictors are independent, and redo the book’s dancing cats example.
