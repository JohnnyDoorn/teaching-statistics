    Exercise 1, part 1 of 5 · single choice · 1 point · 1-spider-a-test.qmd

    Question

    Is arachnophobia (fear of spiders) specific to real spiders or will pictures of spiders evoke similar levels of anxiety? Twelve arachnophobes were asked to play with a big hairy tarantula with big fangs and an evil look in its eight eyes and at a different point in time were shown only pictures of the same spider. The participants’ anxiety was measured in each case.

    Download the data. The data set contains three variables:

    • id - participant name
    • picture - anxiety after the picture of the spider
    • real - anxiety after the real spider

    Every person is measured once with the picture, and once with the real spider.

    What is the appropriate statistical test to find out whether anxiety is higher for real spiders than pictures?


    1. Paired samples t-test
    2. Independent samples t-test
    3. One sample t-test

    Solution

    We have 12 arachnophobes who were exposed to a picture of a spider (picture) and on a separate occasion a real live tarantula (real). Their anxiety was measured in each condition (half of the participants were exposed to the picture before the real spider while the other half were exposed to the real spider first). The same participants were used in both conditions, so the paired samples t-test.

    1. No, there is no significant difference between the picture and the real spider.
    2. No, the picture led to significantly greater anxiety than the real spider.

    Solution

    The two-tailed probability for the spider data is very low (p = 0.031) and significant because 0.031 is smaller than the widely-used criterion of 0.05. The fact that the t-value is a negative number tells us that the first condition (the picture condition) had a smaller mean than the second (the real condition) and so the real spider led to greater anxiety than the picture. Therefore, we can conclude that exposure to a real spider caused significantly more reported anxiety in arachnophobes than exposure to a picture, t(11) = -2.47, p = .031.

    On average, participants experienced significantly greater anxiety with real spiders (M = 47.00, SE = 3.18) than with pictures of spiders (M = 40.00, SE = 2.68).

A psychologist was interested in whether backward masked messages could have an effect. He created a version of Taylor Swift’s ‘Shake it Off’ that contained the masked message ‘deliver your soul to the dark lord’ repeated in the chorus. He took this version, and the original, and played one version (randomly) to a group of 32 veterinary students. Six months later he played them whatever version they hadn’t heard the time before. So each student heard both the original and the version with the masked message, but at different points in time. The psychologist measured the number of goats that the students sacrificed in the week after listening to each version.

  12. EXAMPLE discoverjasp Smart Alex 9.9 - backward messages (paired t-test)
    Exercise 3, part 2 of 2 · open question · 3 points · 3-darklord-b-essay.qmd

    Question

    Based on the t-test output and alpha of 0.05, interpret the output for the researchers, and what this means for their null/alternative hypothesis (1 point). Explain in your own words what the test statistic (1 point) and p-value (1 point) mean and how they inform the decision making process.

    Max 150 words.


    Solution

    The test statistic quantifies the difference between what the null hypothesis posits, and the observed data. The test statistic is standardized, so that it moves away from the measurement scale and follows the t-distribution. The p-value is the probability of the observed test statistic, or more extreme, if the null hypothesis would be true. Here, the alternative hypothesis is two-sided, “or more extreme” means higher and lower t-statistics, which results in a p-value of 0.010, which is significant for alpha of 0.05. We therefore reject the null hypothesis and conclude that fewer goats were sacrificed after hearing the backward message (M = 9.16, SE = 0.63), than after hearing the normal version of the song (M = 11.50, SE = 0.78), t(31) = -2.76, p = 0.010.

    DSUJ §2.10.5, p. 92; §2.10.6, p. 93; §9.9, p. 384. discoverjasp: Task 9.9, results.

    Criteria

    • 0.5 pt: test statistic, mentioning difference between data and model (or any version of that)
    • 0.5 pt: test statistic, mentioning standardization and/or sampling distribution
    • 0.5 pt: p-value, for if the null hypothesis is true
    • 0.5 pt: p-value, probability of test statistic or more extreme
    • 0.5 pt: decision, rejecting the null hypothesis, fewer goats sacrificed after the backward message
    • 0.5 pt: decision, two-sided test
