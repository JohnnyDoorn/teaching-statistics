  1. EXAMPLE SSR - furniture assembly (factorial ANOVA in JASP)
    Exercise 1, part 1 of 4 · open question · 1 point · 1-furniture-a-interaction.qmd

    Question

    A furniture company wants to know whether video instructions help people assemble a bookcase faster than written instructions, and whether that depends on age. In total 120 participants, 40 in each age group (18-35, 36-60 and 61-80 years), are randomly assigned to written or video instructions (instruction; age group is age). The dependent variable is the time needed to assemble the bookcase, in minutes (minutes). Use \(\alpha = .01\) throughout this question.

    The data are available as .csv file.

    Does the effect of the type of instruction on assembly time depend on the age group? Say which result in the output you base this on. (1 point)


    Solution

    Yes. The interaction between instruction and age is significant, F(2, 114) = 13.39, p < .001. The descriptives plot shows how. With written instructions assembly takes longer the older the participants are (29.05, 34.85, 43.60 minutes), with video instructions it hardly changes (28.15, 27.25, 30.25 minutes). So the time that video saves grows with age.

    Other output elements count as well, when used correctly. The descriptives plot (the lines are not parallel), the simple main effects or the conditional post hoc tests can show that the effect of instruction differs between the age groups, with video saving 0.90, 7.60, 13.35 minutes for the three age groups. A significant effect in some age groups and a non-significant one in another does not by itself show that the effect depends on age; that needs the effects to differ.

    Lecture 17, slides 26 (Interaction), 27 (P-value), 32 (Post-Hoc) and 37 (Recap).

    Criteria

    • 0.5 pt: yes, based on the interaction term instruction * age, F(2, 114) = 13.39, p < .001 (not a main effect), or on the descriptives plot, simple main effects or conditional post hoc tests, used correctly (showing that the effect of instruction differs between age groups, not only that it is significant in some and not in another).
    • 0.5 pt: describes how, for instance that the difference between video and written instructions is larger for older participants, or that written instructions take longer with age and video instructions do not.

  2. EXAMPLE SSR - furniture assembly (factorial ANOVA in JASP)
    Exercise 1, part 2 of 4 · tick-all (multiple response) · 1 point · 1-furniture-b-simple-effects.qmd

    Question

    Run simple main effects of the type of instruction, for each age group.

    Check the statement(s) that is/are true; at least one statement is true.


    1. For participants aged 61-80, the type of instruction makes a significant difference in assembly time.
    2. For participants aged 36-60, the type of instruction makes a significant difference in assembly time.
    3. For participants aged 18-35, the type of instruction makes a significant difference in assembly time.

    Solution

    In JASP, Simple Main Effects with instruction as the simple effect factor and age as the moderator. Each test uses the residual mean square of the full model (29.00 on 114 df).

    age 18-35 : F(1, 114) =   0.2793, p = 0.5982  not significant
age 36-60 : F(1, 114) =  19.9151, p < .0001  significant
age 61-80 : F(1, 114) =  61.4495, p < .0001  significant

    For 61-80 and 36-60 year olds the type of instruction makes a significant difference, for 18-35 year olds it does not (p = .598).

    Lecture 17, slide 37 (Recap: simple main effects).


    1. True.
    2. True.
    3. False.

    Question

    What is the F-value of the main effect of the type of instruction? Use 2 decimals.


    1. F-value:

    Solution

    In the ANOVA table in JASP, the row for instruction gives F(1, 114) = 54.87, p < .001. The F-value is the mean square of instruction divided by the residual mean square, 1591.4083 / 29.0031 = 54.87.

  4. EXAMPLE SSR - furniture assembly (factorial ANOVA in JASP)
    Exercise 1, part 4 of 4 · open question · 1 point · 1-furniture-d-colleague.qmd

    Question

    A colleague sees that the main effect of the type of instruction is significant, and concludes that video instructions make everyone assemble the bookcase faster. Do you agree? Why (not)? (1 point)


    Solution

    No. The main effect is an average over the three age groups, and the significant interaction qualifies it. The effect of the instructions depends on age. Video instructions are faster for 36-60 and 61-80 year olds, but for 18-35 year olds there is no evidence of a difference (28.15 versus 29.05 minutes, simple effect p = .598). So the conclusion holds for the older age groups, not for everyone.

    Does not depend on the previous answers; the descriptives, the plot or the simple main effects all support the second criterion.

    Lecture 17, slide 26 (Interaction) and slide 37 (Recap).

    Criteria

    • 0.5 pt: no, the main effect averages over the age groups and is qualified by the (significant) interaction, shown by its F-test or, used correctly, by the descriptives plot, simple main effects or conditional post hoc tests.
    • 0.5 pt: for 18-35 year olds there is no evidence that video instructions are faster (simple effect p = .598, or means of 28 and 29 minutes); the advantage is found in the older age groups.
